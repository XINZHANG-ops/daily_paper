Paper 1

Training Object Permanence in World Models

Published: 2026-09-22

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28654

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: The paper lies at the intersection of video generation models (as candidate world models) and core cognition/developmental psychology, focusing on training and evaluating object permanence and object solidity in AI video systems.
2. 💡 Previous Research and New Ideas: It builds on cognitive science foundations (Baillargeon's violation-of-expectation, Spelke's core knowledge, Piaget's sensorimotor development) and frontier video diffusion models (Sora, Veo, Kling, Wan, Cosmos), filling gaps left by existing benchmarks which are mostly 2D, rely on VLM judges with core-knowledge deficits, and lack training splits. The new idea is that cognitively principled 3D synthetic data can be used to *train* physical reasoning into continuation-based video models.
3. ❓ Problem: Current video generation models systematically violate object permanence (objects vanishing or teleporting behind occluders) and object solidity (passing through barriers, suspending after support removal), and no prior benchmark provides 3D, training-corpus-based, human-judged evaluation of these capacities in the video-to-video setting.
4. 🛠️ Methods: The authors construct 150 hand-authored Blender generators across six task families (Baillargeonian Occlusion, Object Static Occlusion, Container Permanence, Baillargeonian Obstruction, Object Drop, Object Collision), producing a 1.5M-sample training corpus and a 300-question evaluation exam; fine-tune PWM-WROP (16B, based on Cosmos3-Nano) on this data using a native-PyTorch stack on AWS Trainium2; and evaluate it against 13 external models via a unified harness, using blind pairwise human ratings fitted with a Bradley–Terry/Elo model.
5. 📊 Results and Evaluation: PWM-WROP ranks 3rd overall (Elo 1679.5) and 1st among true-continuation models, ahead of the next continuation model by ~224 Elo points; the top two slots are held by reference-to-video systems (Wan 3.0 Prime and MiniMax H3, tied at 1723.6). At matched 320×192 resolution, PWM-WROP achieves the best LPIPS (0.081), MS-SSIM (0.921), and MSE, while qualitative analysis shows it correctly preserves object identity/count through occlusion and tracks support-contingent gravity, whereas baselines exhibit "representation dropout" and "causal decoupling" failures.

2. 💡 先前研究与新思路: 基于认知科学基础（Baillargeon 的预期违反实验、Spelke 的核心知识、Piaget 的感知运动阶段）以及前沿视频扩散模型（Sora、Veo、Kling、Wan、Cosmos），填补了现有基准多为 2D、依赖具有核心知识缺陷的 VLM 评判者、且缺乏训练集的空白；新思路在于可利用认知科学的 3D 合成数据对续生成型视频模型的物理推理能力进行训练。

4. 🛠️ 方法: 作者构建了 150 个手写的 Blender 生成器，覆盖六类任务族（Baillargeonian 遮挡、静态遮挡、容器永久性、Baillargeonian 阻碍、物体下落、物体碰撞），生成了 150 万样本的训练语料和 300 题的评测考试；基于 Cosmos3-Nano 微调出 16B 的 PWM-WROP，使用 AWS Trainium2 上的原生 PyTorch 栈训练；通过统一推理框架对其与 13 个外部模型进行盲比较对评估，并采用 Bradley–Terry/Elo 模型拟合人类偏好。

Paper 2

SpeakerMem-R1: Speaker-Centered Dual-Track Memory for Multi-Party Dialogue

Published: 2026-09-21

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26780

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: This paper focuses on long-term conversational memory for multi-party dialogue, specifically designing speaker-centered memory systems for LLM-based agents operating in group chat settings.
2. 💡 Previous Research and New Ideas: The work builds on existing memory frameworks (Mem0, A-MEM, HippoRAG, Zep, MemOS, etc.) and multi-party dialogue benchmarks (GroupMemBench, SocialMemBench, EverMemBench), as well as RL-based memory management (Memory-R1, Memory-R2). The new idea is a dual-track memory that preserves traceable verbatim messages alongside provenance-linked derived states organized into person-level and group-level views, combined with a speaker-conditioned training approach.
3. ❓ Problem: The paper addresses two core bottlenecks in multi-party dialogue memory: (1) message attribution and relational understanding (distinguishing who said what, whom content concerns, and personal/group scope), and (2) state reconstruction from interleaved histories across members, groups, and time.
4. 🛠️ Methods: The method uses (a) a dual-track memory with System 1 (verbatim messages) and System 2 (four derived layers: person Core/Profile and Group Interaction/Insight); (b) an Anchor–Separate–Resolve–Compose query procedure; (c) SpeakerLevenshtein matching for owner-level structural evaluation; and (d) speaker-conditioned LoGo-GRPO reinforcement learning to train a locally deployable Qwen2.5-3B Writer.
5. 📊 Results and Evaluation: SPEAKERMEM-R1 achieves binary accuracies of 47.9%, 69.2%, and 61.9% on GroupMemBench, SocialMemBench, and EverMemBench respectively, outperforming the best mainstream baselines by 3.3, 12.4, and 9.4 percentage points. On the public EverMemBench leaderboard, it reaches 62.33%, the best reported result. RL training raises the SFT Writer's accuracy from 57.38% to 68.20% (within 3.28 points of the 71.48% LLM writer reference), and ablations confirm the complementary roles of both tracks and structured views.
1. 📘 主题与领域: 该论文聚焦于多方对话中的长期对话记忆，具体为面向LLM智能体在群聊场景下设计的以说话人为中心的记忆系统。
2. 💡 先前研究与新思路: 该工作建立在现有记忆框架（Mem0、A-MEM、HippoRAG、Zep、MemOS等）和多方对话基准（GroupMemBench、SocialMemBench、EverMemBench）的基础上，并借鉴了基于强化学习的记忆管理方法（Memory-R1、Memory-R2）。新思路是采用双轨记忆机制，既保留可追溯的逐字消息，又将带溯源信息的派生状态组织为个人级和群组级视图，并结合说话人条件化训练方法。
3. ❓ 问题: 论文解决多方对话记忆中的两个核心瓶颈：（1）消息归属与关系理解（区分谁说了什么、内容涉及谁、个人/群体范围）；（2）从跨成员、跨群体、跨时间的交织历史中重建状态。
4. 🛠️ 方法: 该方法采用（a）双轨记忆，包含System 1（逐字消息）和System 2（四个派生层：个人Core/Profile和群组Interaction/Insight）；（b）锚定-分离-解析-组合（Anchor–Separate–Resolve–Compose）的查询流程；（c）用于所有者级结构评估的SpeakerLevenshtein匹配；以及（d）说话人条件化的LoGo-GRPO强化学习，以训练本地可部署的Qwen2.5-3B Writer。
5. 📊 结果与评估: SPEAKERMEM-R1在GroupMemBench、SocialMemBench和EverMemBench上分别达到47.9%、69.2%和61.9%的二元准确率，比最佳主流基线分别高出3.3、12.4和9.4个百分点。在公开EverMemBench排行榜上达到62.33%，为最佳报告结果。强化学习训练将SFT Writer的准确率从57.38%提升至68.20%（与LLM Writer参考值71.48%仅差3.28个百分点），消融实验证实了两条轨道和结构化视图的互补作用。

Paper 3

Just-in-Time Memory: Learning to Curate Task-Adaptive Memory for LLM Agents

Published: 2026-09-22

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27334

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: Agentic memory systems for LLM agents, focusing on how and when memory should be curated to maximize future task performance.
2. 💡 Previous Research and New Ideas: Builds on prior write-time memory methods (e.g., Reflexion, ExpeL, Voyager, ReasoningBank, SkillOS, MemP) that distill trajectories into fixed artifacts at task completion; the new idea is to defer curation to read time so the curator can condition on the actual downstream task.
3. ❓ Problem: Write-time memory irreversibly discards information before the future query is known and produces query-independent summaries that must serve many tasks, while learning such curators suffers from a long-horizon credit-assignment problem.
4. 🛠️ Methods: Stores raw trajectories losslessly in a streaming memory bank, retrieves top-k relevant ones via BM25, and uses a trainable memory curator that synthesizes a compact, task-adaptive natural-language payload conditioned on the current task, trained with GRPO using immediate task success as reward while keeping the executor frozen.
5. 📊 Results and Evaluation: Across ALFWorld, WebShop, and τ²-bench, JITMEM improves over the strongest baselines by 16.2, 16.3, and 3.9 absolute success-rate points respectively, with even an untrained curator being competitive; it also reduces input tokens by 50.3–56.3% and executor steps by 28.4–31.4%, and the trained curator transfers across executor models without retraining.
1. 📘 主题与领域: 面向大语言模型智能体的智能体记忆系统，研究记忆应如何以及在何时进行整理（curation）以最大化未来任务表现。
2. 💡 先前研究与新思路: 基于已有的"写入时整理"记忆方法（如 Reflexion、ExpeL、Voyager、ReasoningBank、SkillOS、MemP 等），这些方法在任务结束时将轨迹提炼为固定工件；本文的新思路是将整理推迟到"读取时"，使整理者能够以当前下游任务为条件进行信息提炼。
3. ❓ 问题: 写入时整理在未知未来查询的情况下不可逆地丢弃信息，并产生与查询无关、需服务多种任务的摘要；同时学习此类整理者面临长程信用分配难题。
4. 🛠️ 方法: 以无损方式将原始轨迹存储在流式记忆库中，通过 BM25 检索 top-k 相关轨迹，并使用一个可训练的"记忆整理者"，其以当前任务为条件合成紧凑的任务自适应自然语言载荷，通过 GRPO 利用即时任务成功奖励进行训练，同时保持执行器冻结不变。
5. 📊 结果与评估: 在 ALFWorld、WebShop 和 τ²-bench 上，JITMEM 分别较最强基线提升 16.2、16.3 和 3.9 个绝对成功率点；即使未经训练的整理者也已具备竞争力；此外输入令牌减少 50.3–56.3%，执行步数减少 28.4–31.4%，且训练后的整理者无需重新训练即可在多种执行器间迁移。

Paper 1 (PWM-WROP) stands apart as a cognitive-science-flavored video world model paper — interesting on its own but lower priority unless world models or object permanence are your focus.
