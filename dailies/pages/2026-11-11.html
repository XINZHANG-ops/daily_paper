Paper 1

Don't Just Trust Your Memory: Evidence-Linked Memory Reduces Memory-Induced Degradation in LLM Agents

Published: 2026-11-09

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14320

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: This paper studies reliable procedural memory for LLM agents, focusing on memory-induced degradation in interactive agentic and tool-use environments. 2. **💡 Previous Research and New Ideas:** While prior methods distill trajectories into rules, workflows, manuals, or reasoning strategies without traceable evidence, WikiTrace links every insight to a compilation and raw source trajectory so agents can reassess its validity and applicability. 3. **❓ Problem:** The paper addresses cases where lessons inferred from binary success or failure labels are wrong, out of scope, or misused and thus turn previously solvable tasks into failures. 4. **🛠️ Methods:** WikiTrace compiles each training trajectory into a diagnosis and at most three insights, freezes an insight–compilation–trajectory corpus, and enables evidence descent through read and bash tools when guidance is incomplete, conflicting, failed, or contradicted at runtime. 5. **📊 Results and Evaluation:** Across five benchmarks and eight suites, WikiTrace achieves the best average score of 90.2 versus 79.5 without memory and 87.0 with identical insights alone, while reducing Regression from 6.5% to 4.5% and increasing Gain.

Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits

Published: 2026-11-09

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14210

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: This paper lies in offline LLM model editing and combinatorial optimization, specifically repairing reasoning loops through sparse BF16 weight modifications formulated as Ising/QUBO problems. 2. **💡 Previous Research and New Ideas:** Building on agent-based weight-space programming, state-tree representations, and QUBO-based quantization, it replaces natural-language agent decisions and dense continuous rewrites with sparse, format-native bit-level edits solved through exact row-wise Ising decomposition. 3. **❓ Problem:** It addresses the failure of Qwen3.5 reasoning models to terminate their loops, seeking a cheaper and more compact repair than an LLM-agent edit without gradients, training, or LLM inference. 4. **🛠️ Methods:** Forward-pass statistics identify target layers and loop/concluding directions, an objective combines loop suppression, productive-token preservation, and sparsity, admissible sign flips and exponent shifts induce row-wise QUBOs, and GPU simulated annealing or simulated bifurcation solves them, with an eight-variable QAOA experiment as proof of concept. 5. **📊 Results and Evaluation:** On Qwen3.5-0.8B, TREE-ISING reaches 54.6% with annealing and 55.1% with bifurcation on MATH-500@4 versus 51.7% for the agent edit and a reported 7.9% baseline, while changing 13% fewer weights, using 2.2× fewer BF16 bit flips, four forward passes, and about $0.90, and it transfers to Qwen3.5-2B by raising GSM8K accuracy from 21.6% to 69.4%, although the quantum experiment neither establishes quantum advantage nor produces a complete model repair.
