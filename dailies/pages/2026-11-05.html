From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies

Published: 2026-11-04

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06918

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: This paper investigates encoder-free native vision-language-action models for robotic manipulation, emphasizing how visual representations are connected to continuous control. 2. **💡 Previous Research and New Ideas:** Unlike conventional VLAs that compress images into a fixed encoder output, NEO-VLA embeds raw pixels directly into a shared vision-language backbone and lets a flow-matching action expert read intermediate representations across multiple depths. 3. **❓ Problem:** The paper addresses how fixed visual endpoints can discard action-critical cues such as local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes, thereby restricting precise and robust manipulation. 4. **🛠️ Methods:** NEO-VLA combines a 2B-parameter, 40-layer encoder-free backbone, convolutional patch embedding, modality-aware attention, Native RoPE, and a configurable multi-layer flow-matching action expert, evaluated through matched backbone comparisons, layer-map ablations, activation interventions, and multiple simulation and real-world benchmarks. 5. **📊 Results and Evaluation:** NEO-VLA reaches 90.9% frozen-backbone and 95.3% fully fine-tuned average LIBERO success, outperforms late-layer readout, achieves 89.7% on LIBERO-Plus and 69.4% on RoboTwin clean-to-randomized evaluation, and leads the tested real-world tasks with 12/20, 17/20, and 13/20 successes.

Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit

Published: 2026-11-03

Link: http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06728

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: This paper applies reinforcement learning to streaming video understanding in multimodal vision-language models, focusing on learning when to respond versus continue observing. 2. **💡 Previous Research and New Ideas:** Building on GRPO-based streaming methods such as MMDuet2, ThinkStream, and StreamPro that primarily redesign trajectory rewards, it proposes making the decision turn the shared unit of credit assignment and loss weighting. 3. **❓ Problem:** Trajectory-level GRPO advantages broadcast one signal across all turns, so earlier successes can reinforce redundant replies and earlier failures can penalize useful follow-ups, while token-mean loss aggregation lets long responses outweigh short waiting actions. 4. **🛠️ Methods:** TurnWise scores silence-completed prefixes to estimate each turn’s remaining return, converts these into group-relative turn-specific advantages with a warm-up-controlled correction, and averages token losses within each turn before averaging across turns, without requiring a learned critic or extra rollouts. 5. **📊 Results and Evaluation:** TurnWise achieves the best aggregate results on ProactiveVQA, OVO-Timing, and StreamingBench with both backbones (43.56 PAUC, 21.59 F1, and 49.43 average for Qwen2.5-VL-3B; 42.44, 11.15, and 58.96 for Qwen3-VL-4B), while ablations show that full correction (α=1) reduces repetition from 15.53% to 5.98% versus credit-only optimization and that 21.20% of decisions change advantage sign under turn-level credit.

1. 📘 Topic and Domain: This paper investigates what multi-turn reinforcement learning improves in LLM agents, focusing on trajectory success, interaction-budget robustness, and local decision quality. 2. **💡 Previous Research and New Ideas:** Building on trajectory-level agentic RL and the Pass@(K,T) capability-boundary framework, the paper introduces matched-state and mechanistic analyses alongside BudgetMix RL, which trains agents across varying interaction budgets. 3. **❓ Problem:** The paper addresses whether multi-turn RL’s higher final-task success reflects uniformly better decisions and whether its gains remain reliable under constrained interaction budgets. 4. **🛠️ Methods:** The authors evaluate Qwen3 models with GRPO, GiGPO, and PPO across interactive benchmarks using Pass@(K,T), matched-state action scoring, error-state recovery tests, likelihood analysis, training-dynamics analysis, process supervision, and budget-mixed RL. 5. **📊 Results and Evaluation:** Multi-turn RL expands the base model’s Pass@K boundary but does not uniformly improve single-step quality, instead specializing in error recovery and later-stage resilience, while its advantage shrinks or reverses under tight budgets and BudgetMix improves constrained-budget performance by 7.5, 5.3, and 4.0 percentage points at 25%, 50%, and 75% of the maximum budget without sacrificing full-budget performance.

Today's Reading Tips 今日阅读推荐

Read TurnWise first: it offers a crisp diagnosis of trajectory-level RL’s credit-assignment problem and a well-ablated turn-level alternative, with strong gains and reduced repetition. Pair it with the multi-turn RL study, which similarly finds that trajectory-level gains do not imply uniformly better local decisions and highlights robustness under tight budgets; NEO-VLA offers a looser parallel by preserving action-critical intermediate information rather than relying on a fixed endpoint.
建议先读 TurnWise：它清晰指出了轨迹级强化学习的信用分配问题，并提出消融充分的逐轮次替代方案，同时显著减少重复。可将其与多轮强化学习论文对照阅读，后者同样发现轨迹层面的提升并不等于每一步决策都变好，并强调预算受限时的稳健性；NEO-VLA 则呈现一种较松散的类比，即保留动作所需的中层信息，而不是依赖固定的末端表征。
